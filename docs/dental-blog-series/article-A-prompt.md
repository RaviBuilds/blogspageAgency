## The reader and the one thing they should take away

A Hyderabad dentist who already has a Google listing, an Instagram page and a WhatsApp number, and wonders whether a website is worth the money and attention. **Takeaway:** a clear mental model (**being found is not the same as being understood**), an honest view of **when a website is not the first fix**, and a **20-minute self-audit** they can do from their phone.

## Angle

Do not hard-sell. The strongest move is honesty: say what each channel is genuinely good at, admit when a website is not the priority, and show the gap a website fills. A sceptical clinician should finish thinking "this person was fair to me." Use the research file (Slides 1, 2, 3, 8 and Part A sections 0 to 4 and 16) as the substance, framed as **Blogspage's framework**, not as research.

## Structure (H2s; adapt wording, keep the order)

1. **The short answer.** In the first 120 words: usually yes, but for a specific reason. Google can help a patient find you; a website helps them understand you. Say what the article will cover. Use the pull-line "being found is not the same as being understood" in your own phrasing.
2. **What each channel does well, and where it stops.** Short bulleted list covering: Google Business Profile and Maps; reviews; Instagram; WhatsApp; directory and marketplace listings (refer to them generically; you may name Practo once as an example of a marketplace but make **no claims about its pricing, terms or data practices**); and the website. For each: one line on what it does well, one line on where it stops.
3. **The information gap: six questions patients ask before they call.** List the six: who are the doctors; what treatments are offered; what does the clinic actually look like; what should I expect; is this clinic suitable for my concern; what makes it different. Explain that when the answers are scattered across platforms the patient has to assemble the picture, and that a clinic that presents them clearly in one place may be the one that gets the call, **not necessarily because it is better**. Label this as our observation and inference, and invite the reader to test it against their own enquiry calls ("ask your reception what new patients say they were unsure about"). **No statistics.**
4. **What a dental website is for: five jobs.** Discover, Trust, Understand, Prepare, Connect (Slide 3). Make each concrete for a dentist: Trust means doctor profiles with recognised qualifications and state registration number, and clinical pages with a named reviewer and review date; Understand means plain treatment explanations and honest "what affects this" notes, never a diagnosis; Prepare means first-visit guidance and support for anxious patients; Connect means Call, WhatsApp and Directions, with urgent concerns pointed to a phone call.
5. **What it should not do.** One short section. A clinic website should inform, not promote: no superlatives, no testimonials or before-and-after galleries, no fake urgency, no guarantees. Explain in two or three sentences why this matters after the NDC advisory of 14 October 2026 and link to **Article B** (`/blogs/dental-website-social-media-rules-india-ndc-advisory`). Do not repeat Article B's detail.
6. **When a website is not your first fix.** Genuinely helpful honesty: if your Google profile is unclaimed or shows wrong hours or phone, fix that first; if WhatsApp messages sit unanswered, fix the reception handoff first (a website cannot compensate for an unavailable or unprepared team: blueprint section 16); if you cannot yet state each doctor's qualifications and registration consistently, gather that first. Frame the website as the layer that **gives all of that a permanent, structured home** once the basics are right.
7. **A 20-minute self-audit you can do from your phone.** A numbered list of 7 checks: search your clinic name on mobile; check Google profile hours, phone and address; search "dentist near me" from your locality and note who appears (and how); look for your doctors' qualifications in one place; send a WhatsApp enquiry as a patient and time the reply; ask someone unfamiliar to find "what to expect at a first visit"; check that your information matches everywhere.
8. **Why it matters in Hyderabad.** Short, concrete, hedged. Where several clinics sit within a few kilometres (examples: Banjara Hills, Madhapur, Kukatpally, Secunderabad), patients compare more easily, so clarity matters more. Practical local details a website can state: languages the team actually speaks (Telugu, Urdu, Hindi, English, only as confirmed), metro access, parking, landmarks. Mark this as our inference. **No local statistics.**
9. **Next step.** Two or three sentences linking the argument to the CTA, then the disclosure sentence, then the CTA.

## Must avoid

- Any statistic about patient behaviour, "percentage of patients who search online", or conversion improvements.
- Disparaging Instagram, Google or marketplaces. The framing is "each is valuable; the website is the one you control".
- Claims about ranking, patients or revenue.
- Restating the whole sales deck; use its ideas, not its slide copy.
