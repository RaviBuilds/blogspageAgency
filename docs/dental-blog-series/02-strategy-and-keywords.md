# Strategy, Keywords and Regulatory Brief: Dental Series for Hyderabad Dentists

Prepared 1 November 2026. Read together with `00-GLOBAL-INSTRUCTIONS.md` and `01-research-source.md`.

---

## 1. Audience and goal

**Reader:** practising dentists and clinic owners in Hyderabad (solo practitioners, two to five chair clinics, and dentists planning a new clinic). **Not patients.**

**What they are quietly asking:** "Do I actually need this?", "Is it safe for my registration?", "How do I get found, and what will it cost me in time?"

**Goal of the series:** earn trust with dentists by being the most careful, most useful source on dental clinic websites in India, then convert through one clear call to action per article (a plain-language conversation about the clinic's digital footprint).

**Voice:** the house style in `BLOGSPAGE_AI_CONTEXT.md` section 12 (concise, confident, no hype, no exclamation marks), adjusted for a clinical, peer-to-peer reader. Respectful. Never alarmist about regulation.

---

## 2. The positioning insight (why this series can win)

Search results for dentist marketing are dominated by generic agency content ("25 ways to get more patients"). A lot of it recommends tactics that the **National Dental Commission's advisory of 14 October 2026** has just put on a watch-list: patient testimonials, before-and-after photos, recorded procedures, self-promotion.

The blueprint's core principles happen to fit this new reality: **evidence instead of adjectives, information instead of promotion, doctor-reviewed content, no fake urgency, no guarantees.** So the series position is:

> **"A dental website that informs without promoting."** Be found, be understood, stay inside the Code of Ethics.

This is the differentiator. It is also the safest way to talk to dentists.

**Things we do NOT know and must say we do not know:**
- Whether showing Google reviews on a clinic website counts as "publishing testimonials" under the advisory. Secondary sources lean cautious. Treat as **unresolved; recommend the dentist confirm with their state dental council.**

- Anything about Telangana State Dental Council enforcement specifics. Do not invent any.

**Hard line for all three articles:** we are a web agency, not a regulator or law firm. Every regulatory section carries a short "not legal advice, confirm with your state dental council or a healthcare lawyer" note, and describes what the advisory says before saying how we read it.

### 4.2 How to validate in about 30 minutes (do this before publishing)
1. **Google Search Console** for blogspage.com: check queries already showing impressions for "dental", "dentist", "clinic".
2. **Google Keyword Planner** (free with an Ads account, no spend needed): paste each primary and secondary term, set location to Hyderabad / Telangana, note relative volume bands.
3. **Autocomplete and People Also Ask** in an incognito window for each primary term. Add any question that appears to the article as an H2 or H3.
4. **SERP check:** if page one is all large publishers or marketplaces, prefer the long-tail variant.

- **"Dental clinic marketing / get more patients" (Hyderabad and India):** saturated by agency blogs and international dental-marketing sites. Generic, tactic-heavy, and often at odds with the new advisory. *Hard to win head-on; win with a different angle.*

In the results I saw, few cover the **dental clinic website** angle in depth. *Best near-term opportunity; time-sensitive.*

## 5. The three articles

### Publishing order (recommended): **B, then A, then C**
B is time-sensitive: the advisory is under three weeks old, and interest in it will fade. A is the evergreen hub the others link to. C is the practical how-to.

Slugs are fixed in advance so the articles can link to each other before all three exist.

| | Slug | Doubt it answers | Funnel role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | `do-dentists-need-a-website` | "Is it worth it?" | Evergreen hub / awareness |
| **B** | `dental-website-social-media-rules-india-ndc-advisory` | "Is it safe for my registration?" | Trust and authority, time-sensitive |
| **C** | `dental-clinic-seo-hyderabad-google-maps` | "How do I get found, and what do I do first?" | Practical how-to / consideration |

**Cross-linking:** every article links to the other two once, in context, at a natural point (not in a "related posts" dump). A is linked from B and C as the explainer of "why a website at all".

### What makes each one distinct
- **A** reframes the question: "being found is not being understood." It is honest about when a website is **not** the first fix (unclaimed Google profile, unanswered WhatsApp). That honesty is the trust-builder.
- **B** is the only one of the three that is news-led. It gives dentists a plain information-versus-promotion test and a **claims register** they can use tomorrow.
- **C** is a Hyderabad-specific local SEO playbook that refuses to promise rankings and respects patient privacy in analytics.

### The call to action (all three)
One closing `ctaBlock` per article, plus an optional mid-article `ctaBlock` only on the longest piece (C). The ask is deliberately small and matches the deck's "natural next step": **a plain-language conversation about the clinic's current digital footprint.** Not "book a demo", not "get more patients". Exact copy is in each article prompt. The primary button opens the AI chat widget (see `BLOGSPAGE_AI_CONTEXT.md` section 10).

### Images (kept deliberately light)
- One **banner placeholder** per article (1600x900) so `mainImage` and `ogImage` exist and nothing breaks. The owner will replace these.
- **One simple diagram** in A and in C where it genuinely helps (the patient path). None in B; its tests are clearer as lists.
- No stock photos of real people or patients. No before-and-after imagery. No scraped images. Every image has meaningful `alt` text.

## 6. Issues in the blueprint to resolve (not for the articles, for you)

1. **Patient stories, "Featured Case" and consent-approved case library** (blueprint sections 10, 11, 24; deck slide 11) sit uneasily with an advisory that names testimonials and before-and-after photos. The articles avoid recommending them. Decide whether to **remove or rework them for the Indian dental offering**, and make sure the sales PDF does not pitch them to dentists.
2. **Review display** ("review presence that is genuine and traceable", deck slide 8) is unresolved under the advisory. Position it as "your Google Business Profile reviews, managed ethically", not as testimonial walls, until the position is clearer.
3. **Instagram "Doctor explains" highlights** (deck slide 10) may brush against the "recording and broadcasting" concern. Keep it as educational content only, with doctor review.
4. **The repo's context file lists "HIPAA-aligned" for Dental & Medical.** HIPAA is a US law. For Indian clinics use **DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025**. Worth correcting on the site.
5. The blueprint is **v1.0, unaudited** and contains **no statistics**. The articles therefore present it as your framework, not as proven research.
6. **Existing 10 posts** still show "No description" in Studio (the earlier "Needs SEO attention" list). The new posts must carry every SEO field so they never join that list.
