## The reader and the one thing they should take away

A Hyderabad dentist who suspects their clinic is hard to find and wants to know what to do, in what order, without being sold a ranking guarantee. **Takeaway:** a **five-step order of work** and a **30-day plan**, plus a clear-eyed understanding of what nobody can promise.

## Angle

Practical, specific, honest. Local SEO content for clinics is full of promises; this piece refuses to make them. It treats the Google Business Profile as the foundation and the website as the layer that **supports** it (research file Slide 8 and Part A sections 4, 13, 14, 16, 18). Keep the ethics thread light but present: reviews and health data are handled carefully, and the reader is pointed to Article B.

## Structure (H2s; adapt wording, keep the order)

1. **How a patient's search actually flows.** In the first 120 words: the usual path is a Google or Maps search, then the Business Profile, then reviews, then the website, then a call, WhatsApp message or directions. State plainly that **no one can promise a position**, because results depend on Google's systems and on competing clinics. Say what the article will give them: five steps and a 30-day plan.
2. **Seven kinds of searches to plan for.** From blueprint section 4.1: brand, treatment, concern, location, doctor, urgency, cost and logistics. One line each with a **clearly illustrative** Hyderabad example (for instance a treatment plus a locality such as Madhapur or Kukatpally, or "dentist open now"). State that the examples are illustrations, **not measured searches**.
3. **Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right.** Verify current official guidance before writing and link to Google's help pages. Cover: claiming and verifying; using the clinic's **real name** (no keyword stuffing); choosing the right primary category; accurate hours including holidays; one phone number; services; **real** photos of the clinic as it is today; one profile per real branch; no fake or virtual locations. Only mention a Google feature if you have confirmed it still exists today.
4. **Step 2: Build the website pages that support the profile.** Genuine branch page(s) only for real branches; doctor pages with recognised qualifications and registration number; treatment pages with honest, doctor-reviewed information; clean internal linking from concern to treatment to doctor to branch to contact. Warn against **mass-produced "treatment + locality" pages** (doorway pages); confirm Google's current spam-policy wording before stating it. Stress consistent name, address and phone everywhere.
   - **Place the optional mid-article CTA here** (copy below).
5. **Step 3: Reviews, handled carefully.** Genuine and traceable; never incentivised (confirm Google's current policy and link it); never reveal treatment details when replying publicly (patient confidentiality). Be honest that the NDC advisory of 14 October 2026 names patient testimonials, and that how clinics may encourage or display reviews is **unresolved**; advise confirming with the state dental council, and link **Article B** (`/blogs/dental-website-social-media-rules-india-ndc-advisory`). Do not recommend featuring review screenshots as promotion.
6. **Step 4: The technical basics that matter.** In plain language: mobile speed, HTTPS, a sitemap, Google Search Console set up and verified, canonical URLs, and structured data that matches what is visibly on the page (for example clinic and doctor information). One sentence on why each matters. Do not oversell schema.
7. **Step 5: Make first contact easy, and make sure reception is ready.** Persistent Call, WhatsApp and Directions; a stated reply time the clinic actually keeps; simple routing at reception (treatment, doctor, branch, urgent); a short quick-reply library by topic. A website can send better-informed enquiries, but it cannot compensate for an unavailable team (blueprint section 16). Hyderabad-specific details worth stating on the site: languages the team actually speaks, metro access, parking, landmarks. Link **Article A** (`/blogs/do-dentists-need-a-website`) once, here or in section 4, for "why a website at all".
8. **Measure without tracking patients.** What to track: calls initiated, WhatsApp clicks by page, directions clicks, Search Console impressions and clicks, top landing pages. What **not** to send to analytics: symptoms, enquiry text, tool answers, anything clinical. Mention the DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025 in one or two sentences, with the timeline uncertainty from strategy R9 if you state dates. No HIPAA.
9. **A 30-day plan.** Week 1: Business Profile and consistency of name, address and phone. Week 2: doctor and treatment pages. Week 3: review process and reception handoff. Week 4: Search Console, measurement, and a first review of what the data says. Numbered list.
10. **What nobody can promise.** A short, candid close: rankings depend on competition and Google; results take time; a strong foundation makes your information consistent, useful and easy to find, and that is all it claims. Then the disclosure sentence and the CTA.

## Must avoid

- Any promise or implication of ranking, patient numbers or revenue. No "rank #1".
- Any search-volume figure or "X% of patients" claim.
- Advice to buy, incentivise or script reviews; fake locations; keyword-stuffed business names.
- Naming real competing clinics or agencies.
- Repeating the whole of Article B; one pointer is enough.
