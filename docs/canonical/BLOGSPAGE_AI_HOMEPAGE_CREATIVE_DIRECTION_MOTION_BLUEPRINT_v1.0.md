# BLOGSPAGE AI — HOMEPAGE CREATIVE DIRECTION & MOTION BLUEPRINT v1.0

**Document status:** Canonical creative-direction layer for Homepage V2.0 refinement  
**Applies to:** `blogspage.com` homepage only  
**Audience priority:** Business owners and non-technical decision makers  
**Primary goal:** Evolve the existing R1 homepage into a distinctive, premium, business-owner-first experience without destabilizing its approved architecture or SEO foundation.

---

# 1. Purpose

The R1 implementation established the correct strategic structure for the homepage:

- business-owner-first positioning
- three service verticals
- website-first education
- real project proof
- solution/industry bridge
- trust/process
- human + AI conversation paths
- future service-hub compatibility
- existing SEO and route protection

R2 does **not** replace that structure.

R2 is the creative layer that turns the structurally correct R1 homepage into a more authored, memorable, premium experience.

The central problem to solve is:

> The current homepage is clear and technically polished, but parts of it still feel like a sequence of well-designed components rather than one continuous brand experience.

R2 therefore focuses on:

- visual hierarchy
- storytelling
- scene transitions
- signature motion
- stronger brand recognition
- less repetitive card/grid presentation
- stronger business-owner empathy
- clearer website-first commercial emphasis
- premium restraint

The objective is **not more sections** and **not more animation**.

The objective is:

> **Fewer, stronger visual ideas that make the existing story feel continuous.**

---

# 2. Non-Negotiable Design Principles

## 2.1 Business language first

The first layer of communication must be understandable to a business owner.

Prefer:

> Get your business online properly.

over:

> Build your digital infrastructure.

Prefer:

> Software your team can actually run the business with.

over:

> Multi-tenant RBAC applications.

Technical language may appear as supporting proof, but should not be required to understand the proposition.

---

## 2.2 Technology is proof, not the product

Technology names should support credibility:

- Next.js
- TypeScript
- Supabase
- PostgreSQL
- Sanity
- n8n
- AI APIs

They should not dominate the opening experience.

The visitor should first understand:

**what Blogspage does**

then:

**what Blogspage can build**

then:

**how Blogspage builds it**

then:

**which technology is involved**

---

## 2.3 One continuous story

The homepage should feel like:

> One business journey

not:

> A collection of sections.

Use recurring visual motifs between scenes.

A visual element introduced in one scene should, where practical, reappear or transform in the next.

Examples:

- the Hero “business” node becomes the central object in the digital-presence scene
- the website node becomes the bridge into Brand & Digital Presence
- the same system line later connects to Applications and AI & Automation
- portfolio stories show that abstract system becoming real products

---

## 2.4 Premium restraint

The visual system is:

> **Bright editorial technology + dark product/system environments.**

The homepage should feel:

- calm
- expensive
- intelligent
- confident
- editorial
- human
- technically capable

Avoid:

- cyberpunk
- giant neon glows
- AI robots
- floating AI orbs
- excessive particles
- generic holographic dashboards
- fake metrics
- constant parallax
- exaggerated 3D
- animation for animation's sake

---

## 2.5 Color hierarchy

Maintain the approved light/dark system.

Light scenes are the default for:

- explanation
- business education
- services
- trust
- process
- content
- conversion

Dark scenes are reserved for:

- Hero
- major product/system proof
- software/product visuals
- terminal
- footer

Brand accent colors remain restrained.

Neutral surfaces must remain visually dominant.

# 3. Primary Brand Idea

The homepage should communicate one underlying thought:

# **Wherever your business is today, we can build the next digital layer.**

The visitor journey:

**Start**

Brand & Digital Presence

↓

**Build**

Applications & Business Software

↓

**Scale**

AI & Automation

This is not necessarily the literal headline or service labels.

It is the underlying narrative relationship between the three verticals.

The visitor should feel:

> “I can start simple.”

and:

> “They can stay useful as my business grows.”

---

# 4. Primary Homepage Narrative

The final experience should be organized into five major narrative acts.

## ACT I — FIND YOURSELF

The visitor sees themselves and understands Blogspage.

## ACT II — SEE WHAT IS POSSIBLE

The visitor understands why a digital presence matters and what Blogspage can add.

## ACT III — SEE THE CAPABILITY

The visitor sees the three verticals and real work.

## ACT IV — TRUST THE TEAM

The visitor understands the process, proof, expectations and client experience.

## ACT V — TAKE THE NEXT STEP

The visitor gets an easy way to start, including human and AI paths.

The technical page may contain more than five React components, but the visitor should perceive only these five narrative movements.

# 5. Scene Architecture

## SCENE 01 — HERO / “BUILD A BUSINESS PEOPLE CAN FIND, TRUST AND USE”

### Purpose

Immediately communicate:

- business relevance
- website/digital presence relevance
- broader software capability
- premium quality

### Desired first impression

> “This looks premium.”

Then:

> “These people can actually help my business.”

Then:

> “They do more than websites too.”

### Headline direction

Preferred conceptual direction:

# **Build a business people can find, trust and use.**

Supporting message:

> We build your brand, website, business software and AI automation around the way your business actually works.

Supporting capability line:

> Branding · Websites · Software · AI & Automation

Primary CTA:

> **Tell Us What Your Business Needs**

Secondary CTA:

> **See What We've Built**

### Important

The final approved copy must be checked against the current homepage data before publication.

Do not automatically replace current copy without owner/content review.

### Visual direction

The dark Hero remains.

The hero system/Blueprint should evolve from an abstract engineering diagram into a business-oriented system.

Visual hierarchy:

**Business**

→ **Brand**

→ **Website**

→ **Customers / Enquiries**

→ **Software**

→ **Automation**

→ **AI**

Technical labels may remain inside the system for supporting credibility.

### Motion choreography

1. The business node appears.
2. Brand and website layers assemble around it.
3. Customer touchpoints connect.
4. The system line moves toward software/operations.
5. Automation/AI appears as a later layer.
6. The final composition settles rather than continuously moving.

Motion must feel like:

> **a business becoming digitally connected**

not:

> **a developer architecture animation.**

### Scroll interaction

As the visitor exits the Hero, one visual connector should visually lead toward the next scene.

Avoid abrupt hard cuts.

### Reduced motion

Display a stable final diagram and remove continuous movement.

### Mobile

Simplify the system.

Prioritize:

Business → Website → Customers

and hide secondary technical nodes when necessary.

# 6. SCENE 02 — “WHERE ARE YOU RIGHT NOW?”

### Purpose

Let the visitor identify themselves immediately.

### Core choices

1. **I don't have a website yet**
2. **I already have a website**
3. **I need more than a website**

### Design direction

Do not make this feel like a standard three-card pricing grid.

Instead, treat the choices like three entry points into the same journey.

The selected/hovered path should alter a central visual object.

Example:

**No website**

→ central blank business presence becomes a professional website

**Existing website**

→ central site gains stronger content/experience structure

**Need more**

→ website extends into software/automation

### Motion

Very subtle:

- card/panel raises
- accent line appears
- central visual changes
- supporting copy cross-fades

Avoid large movement.

### No-interaction rule

All three options must still be completely understandable without hover/click.

# 9. WEBSITE-FIRST EMPHASIS

This is a strategic priority because the majority of expected local demand is website/branding-led.

The homepage should make the visitor who needs a website feel:

> **“This company is for me.”**

while making the software/AI visitor feel:

> **“They can do much more than websites.”**

This asymmetric emphasis is intentional.

Do not make the three verticals appear commercially identical if that causes the primary audience to lose recognition.

A premium homepage should not use identical vertical spacing between every section.

### Dark Hero
Longer visual immersion.

### Light explanation
Medium height.

### Visual story
Large breathing room.

### Three verticals
Medium.

### Portfolio
Long dark product island.

### Trust/process
Quieter and shorter.

Large emotional breathing room.

The visitor should feel:

> **acceleration → understanding → proof → calm → decision**

rather than:

> **same card grid repeated.**

# 22. Card Usage Rules

Cards are allowed when they improve comprehension.

Use cards for:

- three service verticals
- selected project metadata
- testimonials
- compact supporting content

- every paragraph
- every process step
- every industry
- every concept
- every CTA

Large composition should be preferred when a visual story can be told better.

# 33. Client / Server Architecture

Keep the homepage mostly server-rendered.

Client components should be introduced only for:

- interaction
- motion requiring browser state
- pointer tracking

- analytics interaction

---

# 34. Performance Rules

Avoid adding:

- heavy video backgrounds
- continuously running particle engines
- large WebGL scenes
- multiple mouse-tracked canvases
- unnecessary client hydration
- giant image payloads

Use:

- optimized Next/Image
- CSS where possible
- Framer Motion where already established
- SVG for lightweight diagrams
- reduced-motion fallbacks
- progressive loading

The creative goal must never reduce Core Web Vitals or make mobile scrolling feel heavy.

---

# 35. Mobile Creative Direction

Mobile is not a compressed desktop.

On mobile:

- simplify the hero system
- reduce node count
- convert horizontal stories to vertical progression
- avoid large pinned experiences
- preserve reading order
- reduce hover-dependent interactions
- use touch-friendly controls
- keep primary CTA visible

Each signature animation needs a static or simplified mobile interpretation.

---

# 36. Accessibility

No important information should depend on:

- color
- hover
- animation
- sound
- pointer tracking

Interactive components require:

- keyboard access
- visible focus
- semantic controls
- accessible names
- appropriate reduced-motion behavior

Decorative diagrams should use appropriate `aria-hidden`.

# 42. Implementation Philosophy

Do not rebuild the homepage from zero.

Start from the R1 implementation.

For every proposed change ask:

> Can the existing component be adapted to express the new creative direction?

Only create a new component when:

- the visual concept is genuinely new
- the existing abstraction would become more complex than a focused new component
- a reusable interaction is required

Avoid component proliferation.

# 44. Design Acceptance Criteria

The homepage should pass these subjective tests.

## Business owner test

A visitor who knows little about technology can answer:

> What does Blogspage do?

within 10 seconds.

## Relevance test

A business owner without a website should think:

> These people can help me.

## Expansion test

A software/AI buyer should discover:

> They are more capable than a normal web agency.

## Trust test

The visitor can find:

- real work
- real reviews
- clear process
- clear contact path

without hunting.

## Distinctiveness test

After seeing the homepage, the visitor can remember:

> “They turn a business into a connected digital business.”

## Motion test

Animations make the story clearer, not noisier.

## Premium test

The site should feel expensive without trying to look expensive.

## Restraint test

No effect should feel added merely because the technology allowed it.

# 45. Hard Prohibitions

During R2 implementation, do not:

- redesign the global color system
- change navbar architecture
- redesign footer
- change SEO files
- rename solution URLs
- remove blog routes
- remove service routes
- rewrite SEO metadata wholesale
- invent testimonials
- invent project metrics
- invent client outcomes
- create fake review schema
- introduce a second animation library without approval
- introduce heavy WebGL
- introduce giant animated gradients
- make the whole homepage client-rendered
- create unnecessary sections
- duplicate future service hubs
- hide important information behind hover

# 46. Final Creative North Star

Every design decision should support this sentence:

# **Start simple. Build properly. Grow from there.**

A small business should feel welcome.

A growing business should see depth.

A technical buyer should see engineering quality.

The homepage should never require the visitor to understand technology before understanding value.

The final impression should be:

> **Blogspage AI understands my business, can build what I need today, and is capable of building what I may need tomorrow.**
